Abstract

Offline reinforcement learning enables reward-driven policy improvement from fixed datasets without requiring online exploration, making it particularly attractive in safety-critical domains. A central challenge, however, is distribution shift: policy optimization may favor actions that are weakly supported by the offline data, rendering value estimates unreliable. Existing approaches primarily control this shift in the policy’s own action space. In interactive environments such as autonomous driving, this can be insufficient: a candidate ego trajectory may remain well supported under the marginal behavior distribution while being poorly supported jointly with the surrounding-agent behavior observed in the logged interaction. We refer to this degradation in interaction support as interaction distribution shift (IDS), and introduce Interaction-Constrained Drive Policy (ICDP), an offline reinforcement learning framework that explicitly controls interaction-level distribution shift. Starting from the joint data distribution over ego and surrounding-agent futures, we show that joint-support degradation decomposes exactly into an ego-support component and a residual interaction-support component. We recover the latter through contrastive density-ratio estimation, isolating interaction compatibility without explicit joint-density modeling, surrounding-agent prediction, or rollouts in reactive simulators or learned world models during policy optimization. Closed-loop evaluations on nuPlan, Interplan and real-world truck experiments show that ICDP suppresses high-value yet interaction-unsupported trajectory selections and improves performance in interaction-critical driving scenarios.

Contributions

  1. Interaction distribution shift. We identify and formalize IDS in interactive offline RL: an ego trajectory can remain well supported marginally while losing support jointly with the surrounding-agent evolution.
  2. Contrastive interaction-support estimation. We derive a density-ratio estimator that isolates the interaction-specific support component, and integrate this constraint with chunk-level value learning and diffusion-policy optimization in ICDP.
  3. Closed-loop and real-world evaluation. We evaluate on nuPlan and InterPlan and deploy on a full-scale autonomous truck, demonstrating suppression of high-value, interaction-unsupported trajectory selections and improved performance in challenging scenarios.

Interaction-Constrained Drive Policy

ICDP combines a diffusion trajectory policy, a pessimistic ensemble of chunk-level critics, and a contrastive interaction-support module. Only the ego policy is optimized; surrounding-agent futures are retained from the logged interactions.

Logged interactions

Learn from scenes containing ego-action chunks, corresponding agent futures, chunk returns, and subsequent states.

2

Candidate generation and value

A diffusion actor proposes trajectories; a pessimistic critic ensemble estimates their long-horizon utility.

3

Contrastive interaction support

The joint-minus-ego classifier score isolates interaction compatibility from marginal ego-trajectory support.

4

Constrained policy improvement

Value guidance is balanced with ego and interaction support penalties and the diffusion behavior-cloning anchor.

Mathematical formulation and optimization details

1 Represent the logged interaction

Each training sample contains a scene s, a logged ego-action chunk τeβ, the corresponding surrounding-agent futures τaβ, a chunk return, and the subsequent scene. Policy improvement must preserve support for the complete ego–agent pairing, rather than only for the ego trajectory.

ΔJ=ΔE+ΔI

Exact joint-support decomposition · Eq. (10)

ΔE=logpD(τeβ|s)pD(τ^e|s)

Ego marginal-support shift

ΔI=logpD(τaβ|s,τeβ)pD(τaβ|s,τ^e)

Interaction distribution shift (IDS)

IDS measures compatibility with observed agent futures. It does not require predicting how those agents would react to a candidate trajectory.

2 Generate and value candidate trajectories

The diffusion actor proposes a candidate ego-action chunk. The critic evaluates the complete chunk, enabling a Bellman target aligned with the temporally extended action. An ensemble lower-confidence-bound estimate combines mean value with a penalty for disagreement, reducing exploitation of uncertain value estimates.

3 Recover interaction support contrastively

The joint classifier distinguishes logged ego–agent interactions from pairings containing policy-generated ego candidates. The ego-only classifier uses the same candidate trajectories as negatives. Subtracting their logits removes the shared marginal ego-support component:

gI(s,τe,τa)=gJ(s,τe,τa)−gE(s,τe)

Residual interaction score · Eq. (11)

The contrastive recovery result and the complete training procedure are presented together in the theory and algorithm card below.

4 Constrain reward-driven policy improvement

The policy balances long-horizon utility with ego-support and interaction-support penalties, while retaining the behavior-cloning denoising objective:

maxθE[QLCB(s,τ^e)−λECE−λICI]−λBCLBC

Interaction-constrained policy objective · Eq. (17)

CI=[ΔI^−ϵI]+

Interaction penalty · Eq. (16)

CE regularizes marginal ego support; CI penalizes interaction shift above tolerance ϵI. The diffusion actor is updated using Noisy Space Policy Gradient (NSPG), with value and support assessed on clean trajectories.

Theorem 1 — Contrastive recovery of IDS

At population-optimal classifier logits, the residual-score difference between logged and candidate ego trajectories exactly recovers interaction distribution shift.

ΔI=gI∗(s,τeβ,τaβ)−gI∗(s,τ^e,τaβ)

Exact recovery at population optimum · Eq. (13)

Distributional interpretation Corollary 2

Fix the state and the logged/candidate ego trajectories. Let qβ and q̂ denote their conditional distributions of surrounding-agent futures:

qβ=p𝒟(τa|s,τeβ)q^=p𝒟(τa|s,τ^e)
Eτa∼qβ[ΔI]=DKL(qβ∥q^)

Expected IDS = conditional KL divergence · Eq. (15)

On average, IDS quantifies the change in the conditional interaction distribution when the candidate replaces the logged ego trajectory. The KL direction is logged → candidate.

Recovery conditions & practical penalty

Exact recovery. Joint and ego classifiers share policy-generated negatives, balanced class sampling, and common support. Both scores use the same logged surrounding-agent futures.

ΔI^=gI(s,τeβ,τaβ)−gI(s,τ^e,τaβ)

Practical IDS estimator · Eq. (14)

In practice. ICDP uses learned logits and the pointwise penalty CI = [Δ̂I − εI]+. The tolerance εI ≥ 0 sets the permitted interaction shift.

Individual IDS values can be negative; their conditional expectation is the nonnegative KL in Corollary 2. The identity applies to true IDS under the support assumptions. A learned estimate and its hinge penalty approximate interaction shift; the penalty itself is not a KL divergence. The distributions are derived from logged data, without predicting counterfactual agent responses.

Implementation. Planning horizon: 32 steps (3.2 s); critic ensemble: 5 heads; interaction context: 48 agents; reverse diffusion: 5 steps; batch size: 1,024; AdamW learning rate: 3 × 10−4. Guidance begins after 5,000 updates; the reported training budget is up to 250,000 updates (Appendix B).

Conclusion

ICDP extends offline policy improvement from marginal ego support to joint interaction support. Contrastive estimation makes this constraint tractable without explicit joint-density modeling or predicted agent responses during policy optimization. Closed-loop benchmarks show consistent improvements, particularly under reactive evaluation, and real-world truck trials demonstrate deployment feasibility. Extending the approach to online reinforcement learning is a direction for future work.
